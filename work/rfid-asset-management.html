04 RFID Asset Management

Asset operations · Connected hardware

Know which assets are leaving, and whether they should.

The system connects RFID-tagged assets with readers at controlled exits, checking recorded movements against custody, checkout status and movement permissions. When a movement breaks the configured rules, it records an incident and triggers the appropriate alerts.

Project focus
Asset custody, movement policies and alarm workflows
Designed for
Asset administrators and security operators

The problem

An asset register cannot tell you what just passed through an exit.

Keeping track of assets means knowing more than who received them. Operators need to understand which assets are recorded inside or off site, what is leaving a building and whether that movement is authorised. The system connects each recorded RFID event with the asset’s custody and movement rules, so operators can investigate an exception with the relevant context.

The working system

How the workflow fits together.

  1. 01

    Identify

    Receive the reader event, resolve the asset and suppress repeated reads.

  2. 02

    Evaluate

    Check the asset’s movement policy, site and active checkout or authorisation.

  3. 03

    Respond

    Record the movement and raise an incident or queue an alarm when required.

  4. 04

    Resolve

    Give operators incident history, acknowledgement and location-correction tools.

Product decisions

Define the boundaries around the work.

Model custody and presence separately

An asset’s assignment, checkout and recorded presence answer different questions. The application keeps those concepts distinct so operators can understand what a movement means.

Treat the response as an operation

The workflow includes grouped incidents, movement rules and notification channels. Recording a reader event is only one part of handling an exception.

Engineering the system

Make the detail hold together.

Make events and commands traceable

The event-processing pipeline validates incoming reads, detects duplicates and records movement decisions. Alarm delivery uses retries and execution acknowledgements.

Connect hardware with the operator workflow

An MQTT event layer links readers and alarm devices to the application. People synchronisation and role-scoped portal tools support the surrounding asset administration.

Proposed extension Agentic AI in the solution

An investigation agent alongside the movement controls.

A proposed investigation agent could assemble the asset’s custody and movement history, explain the configured rule behind an alert and prepare follow-up tasks for an operator. The existing movement rules would continue to control the immediate alarm response.

Reconstruct the incident

The agent would retrieve the asset’s assignment, checkout, movement history, applied policy and alarm delivery state to build a chronological account.

Recommend an investigation step

It could identify missing context, summarise the evidence and draft a task for the appropriate operator, with references back to the recorded events.

  1. 01

    Observe

    The movement system records an incident.

  2. 02

    Investigate

    Retrieve custody, policy, event and response records.

  3. 03

    Propose

    Prepare an incident explanation and an investigation task.

  4. 04

    Resolve

    The operator reviews the evidence and records the resolution.

Proposed extension, not an implemented feature of the reviewed platform. Deterministic rules would continue to control movement and alarm responses; the agent would not silence alarms or authorise asset removal.

In practice

What the system enables.

Operators can connect a detected asset with its custody, checkout and movement permissions, review the incident behind an alert and follow the response through to resolution. Recorded presence and movement history provide context for investigating where an asset should be.

The application implements the movement and alarm workflows. Full reader-to-device physical acceptance is a separate commissioning step; repository evidence does not establish a completed production hardware rollout.
